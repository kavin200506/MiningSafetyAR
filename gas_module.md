🟡 Gas Leak — Worker POV

Normal work

The worker is underground doing a normal task.

He has:

🟢 Multi-gas detector attached to his belt
🟢 SCSR carried as part of his emergency equipment

Then suddenly:

BEEP! BEEP! BEEP!

The gas detector gives an alarm.

1. Worker recognizes the warning

The worker doesn't immediately put on the SCSR.

First:

Stop work → recognize alarm → check detector → alert/communicate the hazard → begin withdrawal according to site procedure.

2. Worker starts withdrawing

The worker moves toward the designated escape route.

This is important because the goal isn't:

"Gas detected → immediately wear SCSR."

It's:

"Gas detected → withdraw toward safety → use the detector to assess the atmosphere encountered along the escape route."

3. Worker encounters the escape path

Now your AR scenario becomes interesting.

The worker reaches a section where the atmosphere may be affected.

He uses the multi-gas detector.

For your simulation, you can show something like:

O₂: Normal
CH₄: Elevated
CO: Normal

or whatever gas/hazard conditions you're modelling.

Then the worker has to make a decision.

4. Two possible branches
🟢 CASE A — Atmosphere remains within the safe conditions defined by your training scenario

The worker:

Checks detector → confirms conditions are acceptable → continues withdrawal → reaches safe/fresh-air area.

No SCSR donning is required in this branch.

This is important because you're teaching:

Don't automatically deploy equipment just because an alarm occurred. Assess the situation and follow the established escape procedure.

🔴 CASE B — Atmosphere is unsafe for escape

The worker checks the atmosphere and determines that the route ahead contains a hazardous atmosphere.

Now:

Stop → use the SCSR according to the approved procedure → continue escape through the hazardous atmosphere → reach fresh air/safe area.

This is where your SCSR becomes meaningful.

The worker isn't using it because:

"Gas leak = SCSR."

They're using it because:

"The atmosphere I must escape through is unsafe to breathe."

That's a much better training concept.
